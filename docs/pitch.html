Flappy Pipe turns five-second price moves into Flappy Bird. The bird is the live price of a Rhea testnet pool, read by our contract on-chain. You shape the gap in the next pipe, tap Drop, and the bird either flies through or hits the pipe.

The gap

Five-second trading is a hit. It isn’t on-chain, and it isn’t fun to read.

Opaque

Tap-trading apps price and settle on their own servers. Players can’t check a single outcome.

Popups

On-chain games ask for a wallet approval on every move. Nobody plays a reflex game through confirm dialogs.

Charts

A candle chart is a terminal, not a game. Most people bounce before their first trade.

Short-horizon “tap the grid” trading in the style of Euphoria is popular, but it runs off-chain. On-chain games feel slow because of wallet popups. And charts scare normal players. We wanted all three fixed at once.

How it plays

Three moves. No chart.

1

The bird is the price

Its height is a live Rhea testnet pool: RFT, ELIXIR or GOLD against wNEAR, sampled on-chain every 1–3 s.

2

Aim the pipe

Tap where you think the bird will be, or drag the gap; − and + set its size. Narrower or farther pays more, up to 100x.

3

Drop

Bird inside the gap at the pipe: stake × multiplier. Bird on the pipe: stake lost.

$0.10 to $1 per pipe · $10 to start · one balance across markets · cash out any time · zero popups while playing

The whole UI is Flappy Bird. A pipe stands every five seconds. You shape the gap in the next one, then tap Drop. The multiplier is shown on the pipe before you commit, and it locks on-chain when the bet lands.

The moment

You know the instant the bird gets there

The bird replays the real on-chain samples a few seconds behind the chain. Each pipe is decided on screen with the contract’s own rule the moment the bird reaches it.

Through: green flash, “+$0.14”, balance up at once

Hit: red pipe, screen shake, “−$0.10”

Then the chain’s settle confirms it, seconds later

Early builds showed results five to ten seconds late, whenever settlement happened, so players never knew what had happened. Now the client runs the same rule on the same samples and shows the result at the exact moment. The chain stays the source of truth: if it ever disagrees, its verdict wins.

Fair by construction

The multiplier prices exactly what you win on

The rule

Each pipe sits on a 5-second window’s end. When a sample lands, the contract prices that point on the straight line between the samples either side, and stores it as the gate. You win if the gate is inside your gap.

The price

Multiplier = 1 ÷ P(gate inside gap), from realized volatility, measured from the last known sample. A 3% house edge, capped at 100x; payouts under 1.05x are rejected.

Same rule, same samples, same line on screen: what you see the bird do is what the contract settles.

Our first rule said the bird had to stay inside the gap for the whole window, but the pricer only modelled the end point, so the odds were quietly worse than shown. We changed the rule to match the price instead of the other way round. Storing the gate price when the sample lands also means a late settle can’t turn a loss into a refund.

Nothing about an outcome lives off-chain. The engine only calls permissionless methods: poke, settle, settle_tourney. If it stops, bets refund rather than lose. Wallet players approve once to add a limited session key; guests get a sponsored testnet account with zero prompts.

Browser

Next.js canvas game. Signs place() locally, reads views pinned to one block.

Engine (Bun, replaceable)

Pokes at 1 Hz, settles every 2 s, pays tournaments at the bell, runs the swap bot and NPCs, sponsors guest accounts. Every job is a permissionless call, so anyone can run one.

The browser and engine both talk only to the contract. The contract talks only to the pool. The client uses the same samples and the same rule as the contract, and reads positions and balance at one block height, because the public RPC is load-balanced across nodes at slightly different heights.

Multiplayer

Pick an entry ($0–$10) and a length (3, 5 or 10 min)

Everyone gets 10,000 points on the same live pipes, with a live rank

Global 🏆 leaderboard: all-time cash P&L, wins, best multiplier, kept on-chain

Tournaments are sessions on the same contract. Points never touch the house vault: the pot is only the entries. Ties split the shares of the places they cover, and with fewer than two players everyone is refunded. These screenshots are from a real two-player tournament on testnet: a tie, so the $2 pot split one dollar each.

Judges should hear the weak spots from us. The biggest is the oracle: testnet pools are idle, so our own swap bot makes the price move, which a mainnet version can’t do. Every cap is enforced on-chain, so the vault’s worst case is bounded today.
